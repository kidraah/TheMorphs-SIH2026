# INSAT-3D/3DR: Indian National Satellite System

## 1. Introduction
**INSAT-3D** (launched 2013) and **INSAT-3DR** (launched 2016) are advanced geostationary meteorological satellites operated by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Stationed at 82°E and 74°E respectively, they provide continuous, real-time monitoring of weather systems over the Indian subcontinent and the Indian Ocean.

## 2. Payloads
- **Imager (6 Channels):** Visible (VIS), Shortwave Infrared (SWIR), Mid-Infrared (MIR), Water Vapor (WV), and two Thermal Infrared (TIR-1, TIR-2).
- **Sounder (19 Channels):** Provides vertical profiles of temperature and humidity through the atmosphere.

## 3. Resolution and Frequency
- **Spatial:** 1 km for VIS/SWIR, 4 km for MIR/TIR, 8 km for WV.
- **Temporal:** Full frame scanning every 30 minutes. In staggered/staring modes (combining 3D and 3DR), data can be retrieved every **15 minutes**, making it indispensable for tracking rapidly developing cloudbursts that traditional 24-hour gauges miss.
