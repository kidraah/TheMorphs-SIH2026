# Detailed Phenomena and Science (Thermodynamics)

IMDAA tracks the fundamental physics that cause severe weather.

## 1. Instability (CAPE and CIN)
- **CAPE (Convective Available Potential Energy):** Measures the positive buoyancy of an air parcel. If you push a parcel of air up, and it is warmer than the surrounding air, it will continue to rise on its own like a hot air balloon. CAPE is measured in Joules/kg. Values > 1500 J/kg indicate severe thunderstorm potential.
- **CIN (Convective Inhibition):** The negative buoyancy capping the CAPE. It’s a layer of stable air that traps the hot, moist air below it. 
- **The Phenomenon:** A "Loaded Gun" sounding occurs when high CAPE is trapped by CIN. The energy builds up dangerously. When a trigger (like wind hitting a mountain, or intense afternoon sun) breaks the CIN layer, the CAPE is released explosively, causing extreme updrafts leading to cloudbursts.

## 2. Vertical Wind Shear
The change in wind speed and direction with height. 
- **Low Shear:** The storm grows straight up. The rain falls right back through the updraft, killing the storm quickly. However, all the water falls in one spot (short, intense cloudburst).
- **High Shear:** The storm tilts. The rain falls away from the updraft. The storm survives longer and becomes a supercell (hail, tornadoes).

## 3. Low-Level Moisture Convergence (LLMC)
IMDAA tracks how much water vapor is being pushed into a specific area by the wind. In the Himalayas, the monsoon winds funnel moisture up narrow valleys. The moisture converges, gets trapped, and is forced upwards, acting as unlimited fuel for extreme precipitation.
