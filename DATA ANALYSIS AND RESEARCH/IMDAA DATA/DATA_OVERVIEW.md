# IMDAA: Indian Monsoon Data Assimilation and Analysis

## 1. Introduction
The **Indian Monsoon Data Assimilation and Analysis (IMDAA)** is a high-resolution regional atmospheric reanalysis dataset specifically tailored for the Indian subcontinent. It is a collaborative effort between the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) (India), the India Meteorological Department (IMD), and the U.K. Met Office.

## 2. Core Specifications
- **Spatial Resolution:** ~12 km (0.108° grid).
- **Temporal Resolution:** 1-hourly output for surface variables, 6-hourly for upper-air fields.
- **Coverage Period:** 1979 to 2020 (with "IMDAA-like" NCUM operational data continuing post-2020).
- **Domain:** Focuses on the Indian monsoon region extending across South Asia, specifically designed to capture the complex topography of the Himalayas.

## 3. The Need for Regional Reanalysis
Global reanalysis datasets like ERA5 (~31 km) or NCEP-NCAR often fail to resolve the fine-scale orographic forcing of the Himalayas. Cloudbursts in Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh are highly localized phenomena heavily influenced by steep valleys. IMDAA bridges this gap by assimilating dense regional observations (including Indian Doppler Weather Radars and local rain gauges) into the Unified Model (UM) 4D-Var data assimilation system.
