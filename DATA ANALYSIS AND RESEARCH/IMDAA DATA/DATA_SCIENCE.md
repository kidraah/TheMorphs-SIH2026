# The Science of IMDAA: Thermodynamics of a Cloudburst

Cloudbursts (>100mm/hr) are driven by specific physics that IMDAA captures mathematically.

## 1. Convective Instability (CAPE vs CIN)
- **CAPE (Convective Available Potential Energy):** Measures the buoyant energy available to accelerate an air parcel upward. In the Himalayas, daytime heating combined with moisture transport can cause CAPE to spike >1500 J/kg.
- **CIN (Convective Inhibition):** The energy barrier preventing parcels from rising. A "loaded gun" sounding occurs when high CAPE is capped by high CIN. When an orographic lift (a mountain) breaks the CIN, the CAPE is explosively released, leading to cloudbursts.

## 2. Low-Level Moisture Convergence
Cloudbursts require immense fuel. IMDAA tracks **Specific Humidity** at 850 hPa. The convergence of moisture-laden winds from the Bay of Bengal or Arabian Sea into the narrow valleys of Uttarakhand creates a localized surplus of precipitable water (IWV).

## 3. Vertical Wind Shear
The difference in wind vectors between 850 hPa and 200 hPa. Weak shear allows a storm to grow vertically and dump all its rain in one spot (stationary cloudburst). Strong shear tilts the storm, prolonging its life but moving the precipitation footprint. IMDAA's precise wind fields are critical for predicting whether a storm will stall over a valley.
